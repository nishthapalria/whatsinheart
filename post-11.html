Entry No. 11 · Beauty

The Ugly Truth About Pretty Privilege: You're More Right When You're Hot

Let's stop lying. It's called the halo effect, and your face is running PR for your opinions.

When you look put together — good skin, clean hair, clothes that fit — people don't just think she's pretty. They think she's competent. She's trustworthy. They agree with her take on crypto and her breakup advice.

The theory is brutal and simple: beauty = credibility.

And if that makes you uncomfortable, good. It should. Because it's true.

1. Your Brain Is Lazy, and Pretty Hijacks It

Humans make thousands of decisions a day. Your brain can't deep-dive every one. So it shortcuts. Pretty = good. Good = right.

It's why news anchors wear blazers. Why CEOs don't show up to board meetings in hoodies unless they're already famous enough to get away with it. Why the girl on TikTok giving tax advice in a full face and blowout has millions of followers, and your CPA with acne doesn't.

The truth: people don't listen to the most accurate person. They listen to the most watchable person. Your skincare routine is now part of your argument.

2. Always Looking Pretty Isn't Vanity. It's Strategy.

I'm not telling you to get work done. I'm telling you the game is rigged, so learn the rules.

When you're put together

"She's so articulate." "Bold take, I love it." "She commands a room." People assume your success.

When you're not

"She's aggressive." "Why is she so angry?" "Who let her in here?" People ask you to prove it.

Same words. Different lip gloss. Different outcome.

This is why "effortlessness" is a lie. The clean girl with no makeup spent forty minutes on "no makeup." The "I woke up like this" CEO has a stylist. Pretty is labor. And labor gets paid.

3. So Yes: Wear the Clothes. Do the Makeup. Fix Your Skin.

I can hear the comments already: this is toxic, you should be loved for your brain.

Cool. "Should" and "is" are different countries. I live in "is."

If your takes on life matter to you, make them harder to ignore:

  1. Clothes: fit over brand. Wrinkled reads as unreliable. Tailoring is cheaper than a bad reputation.
  2. Makeup: you don't need a full beat. You need to look intentional. Brows done reads as "she has her life together."
  3. Skin: you're not trying to look 22. You're trying to look like you sleep and drink water. People trust hydrated humans.

The truth: this isn't about the male gaze. It's about human bias. Women judge you harder than men do. Your boss does it. Your mother-in-law does it. You do it.

4. The Guilt You Feel? That's the Scam.

They told us "don't care about looks" while simultaneously promoting the girls who did. They told us "beauty standards are fake" while giving talks in heels.

The scam is making you feel vain for playing a game you didn't create but still have to survive.

So drop the guilt. Pick up the concealer. Or don't — but know what that choice costs. You can opt out of makeup. You can't opt out of people's perception.

The bottom line: pretty privilege is real. Unfair? Absolutely. Ignorable? Not if you want to be heard.

Your ideas deserve armor. Sometimes that armor is a blazer. Sometimes it's clear skin. Sometimes it's red lipstick that makes you sit up straighter when you talk.

Wear the pretty clothes. Take care of your skin. Not because you're insecure. Because you're strategic.

The world listens to people it likes looking at. So give them something to look at while you tell them the truth.

Hate the game or play it better. But don't pretend it's not happening.
