I Am Obsessed With Dharma Movies And If You're Not, You Need To Get Your Priorities Straight

Let's get one thing straight: Dharma movies are not just movies. They're therapy. They're a blueprint.

They're the reason you think love should come with a violin solo and a rain sequence.

If you grew up believing your life should have a Yash Chopra filter, SRK monologues, and Alia Bhatt eyeliner, congratulations. You're my people.

And if you don't get it? Babe. We need to talk. Here's why being obsessed with Dharma movies is actually a personality trait:

1. You Don't Want a Boyfriend, You Want a Karan Johar Hero

He doesn't just text "wyd." He shows up at the airport. He says "pyaar dosti hai" and actually means it. Your standards aren't high. Bollywood raised them.

2. You Think Every Fight Should End With a Monsoon

Healthy communication? Sure. But also — where's the dramatic rain? The white kurta? The running-through-the-street-crying moment?

3. You've Planned Your Wedding Lehenga Since 2008

Kabhi Khushi Kabhie Gham ruined you. Now every family function is just an audition for your eventual grand entry. You don't walk into rooms. You descend the staircase. Chiffon dupatta mandatory.

4. You Believe in Delusional Self-Love Because Poo Did

"It's my look, it's my style, and I love it." Poo was the first girl's girl. She was loyal, and would fight for her family in couture. If you're not channeling Poo energy at least once a week, what are you doing?

5. You Think Every Life Problem Can Be Solved With a Glow-Up Montage

Heartbreak? New haircut plus a sad-then-triumphant song. Dharma taught us the algorithm: pain → makeup → comeback. And honestly? It works.

6. You're Not Crying, You're Just Hydrating Your Eyes for the Climax

Dharma movies taught you that crying is cinematic. You let the tears fall while staring out a sunset window. Bonus points if the right song plays in your head.

7. You Know Your Worth Because Kajol Yelled It First

"I'm beautiful, I'm smart, and don't you dare forget it." Every time a man tries to dim your light, you hear Kajol in your head. Dharma didn't just give us romance. It gave us rage. And lip liner.

The bottom line? Being obsessed with Dharma movies isn't cringe. It's aspirational.

It means you want love that's loud. Friendships that are forever. Outfits that deserve their own song.

The world keeps telling you to be realistic. Dharma told you to be iconic.

So wear the glitter. Demand the airport run. Cry in the rain. Your life deserves a soundtrack.

Now tell me: which Dharma movie raised you? I'll go first — Kal Ho Naa Ho taught me that even the messy parts deserve violins.
