12 Reasons You Need To Be A Girl's Girl (And If You're Not, You're The Problem)

"I am not like other girls" is not the flex you think it is. You are like other girls. We all are. And that's the point.

Being a girl's girl isn't about friendship bracelets and brunch. It's about loyalty. It's about safety. It's about choosing women over male validation every single time. Here's what it actually looks like:

  1. You stop seeing other women as competition. Her promotion is not your demotion. Her glow-up doesn't dim your light.
  2. You guard her drink like it's your job. She wants to dance all night? Your job is to hold her purse, walk her to the bathroom, and make sure no creep tries anything. That's sisterhood.
  3. You hold her accountable because you actually care. Not tearing her down — but calling it when she's settling. "Girl, he's 32 and lives with his mom. You deserve better." That's love.
  4. You support her version of femininity, not just yours. Stay-at-home baker or CEO — you hype either. Not your job to get it. Support her anyway.
  5. You choose her safety over his ego, every damn time. Guy's being creepy at the bar? "Hey! She's with me." Doesn't matter if you know her. Girls protect girls.
  6. You tell the girlfriend — yes, even if he's your friend. The sisterhood comes before the bro code. You'd want to know if you were her.
  7. You hand out tampons if they run out. Bathroom stall solidarity is non-negotiable.
  8. You tell her about the spinach in her teeth. Quietly. Quickly. Before she walks into that meeting.
  9. You shut down the interrupters. "Sorry, she wasn't done. Continue." Amplify her voice.
  10. You decenter men, immediately. The less you perform for male approval, the happier you'll be. You're not the supporting character in his story. You're the whole plot.
  11. You realize you don't have to like her to have her back. This isn't about being besties with every woman. It's basic respect.
  12. You're done with pickme behavior. Putting other women down to make men like you is embarrassing for you. Strong women don't need to step on other women to feel tall.

The bottom line? After years of society pitting us against each other, choosing the sisterhood is a radical act.

A girl's girl doesn't choose a man's comfort over a woman's safety. Ever.

So if you're still asking "am I a girl's girl?" — check yourself. If you do this stuff, you are. If you don't? Start now. We need you.
