Did the Pink City Princess Play It Too Safe at the Met Gala?

A look at Gauravi Kumari's tribute to Maharani Gayatri Devi, and why it felt more like a wedding guest than Costume Art.

Gauravi Kumari is, without a doubt, a total vision. She has that natural, regal "it" factor that you just can't buy.

For the 2026 Met Gala, where the theme was Costume Art, she went down the sentimental route: a vintage pink chiffon saree that actually belonged to her grandmother, the iconic Maharani Gayatri Devi. Designer Prabal Gurung took that sixty-year-old fabric and reworked it into a modern gown — Jaipur pink, dripping in layered pearls and rubies.

On paper, it sounds like a perfect old-money tribute.

But if we're being honest? It felt like a missed opportunity.

The Met Gala isn't just a red carpet, it's a high-fashion playground. But this look felt a little too safe. Instead of global fashion icon, it was giving rich guest at a destination wedding. At a cocktail party or a sangeet, I'd say she looked lovely. For the Met? It felt a bit pedestrian.

Is it just me, or was the look giving major Menaka-and-Shakuntala vibes — not in a cool, edgy way, but more like a costume from an old mythological TV show? It felt like she was cosplaying royalty rather than actually being the modern royal she is.

Where Was the Jaipur Art?

This is the part that stings. Jaipur is a goldmine of craftsmanship. They could've done something mind-blowing inspired by the fresco art of the City Palace. Even a sharp, modern take on a leheriya saree would have had more art than this.

Even the styling felt a bit confused — the hair made me wonder if she was going for a Raja Ravi Varma painting look, but it just didn't quite get there.

We've all seen Gauravi in her traditional poshak looks, and let's be real, she looks ten times better in those. She comes from a land where there is art in every nook and corner, so to see her play it this safe is a bit of a letdown.

Prabal Gurung usually hits the bullseye, but this felt like a rare miss. It's beautiful to honor your family's history, but for the Met Gala, you have to bring the drama. This was a lovely tribute — it just wasn't Costume Art.
