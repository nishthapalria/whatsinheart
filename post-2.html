Entry No. 2

A Theory About Kitchens

You can tell how someone thinks by watching where they put the spoons.

Every kitchen I've ever loved has had its own strange logic, and none of that logic is written down anywhere. The spoons live in a drawer that also holds a single candle and a roll of tape. The good knife is not the sharpest one. There is a bowl by the door that has held, at various times, keys, a dead phone, three limes, and once, inexplicably, a single glove.

I've started to think this is where you learn the most about a person — not from what they say about themselves, but from the small, unexamined decisions they've made about where things go. Nobody designs a kitchen from first principles. You just start putting things somewhere, and eventually somewhere becomes the only place that makes sense, and you couldn't explain why if you tried.

My own kitchen has a shelf that exists for no reason except that it's where the good olive oil ended up one day, and now it would feel wrong anywhere else. I've moved twice since then. The shelf has moved with the reasoning intact, even when the actual shelf is a different shelf entirely.

I think this is true of more than kitchens. We all have a few of these — arrangements that stopped being decisions and became just how it's done. Half the work of knowing someone is finding out which ones they have, and why the glove is still in the bowl.
