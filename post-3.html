Entry No. 3

I Unlearned Being a Hopeless Romantic and My Life Got 10x Better

Nobody tells you about the high. Not the can't-sleep-because-your-brain-is-daydreaming kind.

For years, I was the expert at daydreaming about the wrong things. I could spend six hours constructing an entire future with a person who took six hours to text back. I knew their coffee order before I knew my own five-year plan. I, the sensitive child, the emotional one, the one who cried on KK's songs in the bathroom.

I poured all that cinematic, obsessive, world-building energy into someone who was, at best, a supporting character.

Then one day, I took all of that energy and pointed it somewhere else. I pointed it at the dream I had before any boy ever told me "you are pretty."

I thought I had no discipline. I thought I was too emotional, too sensitive, too distracted to ever really build something.

I wasn't. I was just exhausted.

It takes so much energy to live inside a love story that isn't happening. To decode texts, to rehearse conversations, to shrink yourself, to wonder. That's a full-time job with no PTO.

When I quit that job, I suddenly had all this time, all this fire, all this focus. And I gave it to my work, my craft, my weird little dream that I've carried since I was twelve.

And it exploded.

I stayed up until 1 a.m. not because I was crying, but because I was building. Editing, writing, designing, planning, learning. And for the first time, the butterflies in my stomach were not for a person. They were for my own life.

I remember how I used to imagine someone watching me. Like, "he would love how passionate I am when I talk about this."

Now there is no imaginary audience. There is just me, in my room, doing the thing. And I realised I don't need anyone to be impressed by my passion.

For us millennials, we were taught that success for a woman who feels too much is finding someone who can handle how much she feels. No one told us that the best place for all that feeling is actually in our work.

I, who once wrote long love letters that never got sent, now write the company proposals. I, who once remembered every single detail about my crush, now remember every client, every idea, every deadline, because my heart is finally in the room.

I stopped waiting to be chosen. I started choosing myself, every single day, at 9 a.m. when I open my laptop.

Romantic love is volatile. One day you're everything, one day you're left on read. Your self-worth goes up and down with someone else's mood.

Career love, your dream career, is not like that. It's slow, loyal, and it compounds.

No one tells you how crazy good it feels when a stranger pays for what your sensitive hands made. When someone says, "this helped me," "I needed this," "you're so good at this." When you look at your bank account and realize your softness, your sensitivity, your daydreamer brain actually built something real.

That first payment you made from your art hits harder than a hundred "you're so pretty" texts. That email that says "we'd love to work with you" will make you scream louder than any love confession ever did.

Because this time, they are not loving the idea of you. They are loving what you did.

I didn't close my heart. I just gave it a better job.

I am still the girl who cries at movies and who feels everything too much. I just don't bleed on people who haven't earned it anymore. I bleed into my work, and my work turns it into gold.

Last evening I closed my laptop. My eyes were tired, my room was a mess, and I felt it again — that absurd, giddy, crazy good feeling bubbling up in my chest.

It is freedom.

It is me, finally living the life that my younger self was daydreaming about all along. I wasn't daydreaming about love. I was daydreaming about becoming myself.
