Entry No. 3

The Long Way Home

On taking the route that doesn't save any time.

There's a way home from the station that takes eleven minutes longer, and I've started taking it on purpose. It goes past the house with the impossible garden, the one that seems to grow a new thing every week whether or not the season agrees. Past the bakery that's never once been open when I've walked by, so that its interior has become a kind of rumor.

The short way is fine. It's a straight line and a crosswalk and a hill that gets less interesting every time. The long way has no particular destination logic — it just wanders past things, in the way a conversation wanders when nobody's trying to make a point.

I think I resisted this for a long time because it felt like wasting eleven minutes, and I was raised to believe minutes were a currency you shouldn't spend on nothing. But I've started to suspect the minutes aren't spent on nothing. They're spent on noticing — which is its own kind of arrival, even if it doesn't get you anywhere faster.

Yesterday the garden had put out something orange I didn't recognize. I don't know what it was. I didn't look it up. I just wanted, for eleven minutes, to be the kind of person who walks past a thing and lets it stay a small mystery.
