I Stopped Fighting My Body and Bought the Damn Shorts. It Saved Me

For years, I dressed like I was apologizing. For thighs that touched. For a stomach that wasn't flat. For arms that waved back.

My closet was a graveyard of "flattering" tunics, high-waisted shapewear, and black linen pants meant to elongate.

I thought I was hiding. Really, I was broadcasting: I don't belong here yet.

Then I had the most radical thought of my adult life: what if I just… wore clothes?

1. The Acceptance Didn't Come From Yoga. It Came From Jeans.

Nobody loves their body into acceptance at a meditation retreat. I got there in a Target dressing room, holding three sizes of the same jean.

The pair that fit? A size bigger than I should be. Straight leg. No tummy panel. No magic stretch.

I bought them. And the world didn't end.

The truth: your body isn't the problem. The size tag is. Cut it off. Wear jeans that don't cut into your organs. You'll walk differently. Because you can breathe.

2. The Uniform That Shut Up the Noise: Jeans. Tank. Shorts.

Once I accepted the shape I had today — not the one I was punishing myself for — I needed a uniform. Something so simple I couldn't overthink it.

3. Dressing For My Body ≠ Hiding My Body

"Flattering" is diet culture in a prettier font.

"Dress for your body type" usually translates to: here's how to look ten pounds thinner and three inches taller so you can trick people into thinking you're already the after photo.

I flipped it. I started asking: what lets me move, sweat, sit, and forget I'm in a body?

Turns out, that's tank tops in summer. That's shorts on vacation. That's jeans that don't require a post-lunch unbutton.

The truth: confidence isn't a size. It's not needing to check the mirror every time you stand up.

4. What Actually Changed When I Stopped Hiding

  1. I went outside more. Turns out heat exists. Shorts help.
  2. I stopped waiting for events. Brunch, beach, date night — I had clothes that fit now. So I said yes now.
  3. My energy came back. Hating your outfit is a 24/7 background app draining your battery. Delete it.

I didn't manifest a new body. I stopped requiring one to participate in my life.

The bottom line: your body isn't a problem to solve before you get dressed. It's the body you have. On Tuesday. In June. At 28 or 32.

Buy the jeans that fit. Wear the tank. Own the shorts.

The people who matter don't care. The ones who do? They're still wearing flattering tunics in 90-degree weather. Let them sweat.

You've got a life to live. Dress like it.
